KUDUNOTES

KUDUNOTES listens to a recording and writes out its notes, as a piano roll and as guitar tabs. Play them back, slow them down, and put right what it misheard.

Or drop a file anywhere on this page. It works best with one or two instruments playing, such as a piano alone or a guitar alone. Everything happens on this device: your recordings are never sent anywhere.

How to use KUDUNOTES

  • Start by opening a sound file (MP3, WAV, M4A, or a video), or by recording with the microphone. KUDUNOTES listens and shows the notes.
  • The piano roll (top) shows each note as a bar beside a keyboard: higher notes are higher up, time runs left to right. The tabs (bottom) show the same notes on six guitar strings as fret numbers.
  • Play with the button or Space. Click the time ruler to jump. Drag along the ruler to repeat a part. Slow it down with Speed; the pitch stays the same.
  • Recording / Notes mixes what you hear: all the way left is the recording only, right is the app's notes only. Listening to both is the quickest way to hear a wrong note.
  • Faint notes hides notes the app wasn't sure about. If real notes are missing, move it left; if there are too many extra notes, move it right.
  • Correcting: click a note to choose it (Shift+click or drag a box for several). Drag it to move it, drag its right end to make it longer or shorter, press Delete to remove it. Double-click an empty place to add a note. Ctrl+Z undoes, Ctrl+Y redoes. Arrow keys nudge the chosen notes.
  • Guitar: choose the tuning, a capo and the highest fret to use. To play a note on another string, choose it and press String ↑ or String ↓ (] and [).
  • Keeping your work: pieces are saved in this browser by themselves. “Save project file” makes one file you can open on another device. “MIDI” and “Tabs as text” are for other programs and for sharing.
  • Scrolling: the mouse wheel moves through time; Ctrl+wheel (or + and −) zooms.

It hears one instrument best. With two instruments, or chords with many notes, expect to correct more. Singing and drums confuse it.
